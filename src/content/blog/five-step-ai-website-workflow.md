Every cohort I teach gets the same warning on day one: **if your first move is opening an AI tool, you've already lost.**

The people who fail to build a website with AI don't fail technically. They fail because they start generating before deciding. So this post is the entire workflow I teach, condensed. Steal it.

## Step 1 — The one-page brief (15 minutes, no AI)

Write down, in your own words:

- **Who** the site is for (one sentence)
- **The one action** a visitor should take (book, buy, call, subscribe)
- **The three things** they must see before taking that action
- **The feeling** of the first screen (calm? bold? luxurious?)
- **Your proof** (photos, testimonials, prices, past work)

This becomes your brief. It fits on one page. If it doesn't, you're writing a novel, not a website.

## Step 2 — Generate the scaffold

*Now* you talk to an AI. Paste your brief and ask for a structure first, not a design. Something like:

```text
You are helping me build a small business website.
Read the brief below and reply with only:
1. A site name suggestion
2. A list of pages/sections in order
3. One sentence describing each section's job
4. What you would intentionally leave out and why

Brief:
<paste your brief here>
```

Why ask for structure first? Because structure is cheap to fix and expensive to discover later. My students who skip this step always end up with a beautiful homepage pointing at nothing.

## Step 3 — Judge like a grumpy customer

Read what came back as if you're the pickiest customer you know. My classes use three questions:

1. Can a stranger tell what we do in five seconds?
2. Is the *one action* from our brief impossible to miss?
3. Does it sound like us, or like a brochure?

Anything that fails gets cut or rewritten — by you, in your words. AI writes in "website voice" by default. Your voice is the differentiator.

## Step 4 — Edit in small loops

One change at a time. Check it on your actual phone. Repeat.

The mistake I see constantly: dumping ten edits into one giant prompt. The output comes back different in ways you didn't ask for, and now you're chasing regressions on a website you can't read. Small loops keep you the author. The moment you can't explain why a change happened, you've handed the pen over.

## Step 5 — Ship at 80%, then garden

Publish before it's perfect. Free hosting means the cost of "slightly wrong on the internet" is zero. A live website gathering real visitors beats a perfect one living in a draft folder.

Then garden: add a testimonial when a customer says something nice. Swap the hero photo when you get a better one. Websites are not statues.

## Why this works

Notice the shape of the whole thing: **decide → structure → generate → judge → refine → ship.** The AI only ever owns two steps. The judgment bracket around it is yours.

That's the real skill I teach — not prompting tricks, but staying in command of a machine that's very eager to help.
